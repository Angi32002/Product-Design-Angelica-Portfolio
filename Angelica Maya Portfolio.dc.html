Playing to understand the brain

An interactive arcade experience designed for Parque Explora that translates the complexity of neural networks into a playful and accessible game.

Parque Explora
Medellín, CO.
Context / Brief

Parque Explora is an interactive science museum in Medellín dedicated to bringing science, technology, and innovation closer to diverse audiences through immersive and educational experiences. As part of the development of a new experience for the Mind room, we were challenged to design an interactive module that combined ergonomics, accessibility, and inclusion.

The proposal focused on creating an experience for people with reduced mobility, while also making the interaction engaging and intuitive for different visitors. This led us to explore how a physical product and a digital game could work together to communicate complex concepts related to the brain in a playful, clear, and accessible way.

My role

My role focused on the programming of the game interface in Python and the definition of the product's physical appearance, ensuring visual coherence with Parque Explora. I also contributed to the 3D modeling, ergonomic development, and physical construction of the arcade module, as well as the 3D rendering and visualization of the final product.

Tools
Python · SolidWorks · Blender · Illustrator
Overview

What happens when the neural networks in our brain slow down?

This question became the starting point for Conexión en Conflicto, an interactive arcade experience designed to make a complex neurological process easier to understand through play and interaction.

The user controls a car traveling along a road that represents a neural network, navigating obstacles that symbolize the deterioration of neural transmission, such as the loss of myelin or reduced neurotransmitter activity. Through this analogy, the experience transforms an abstract scientific concept into a playful, visual, and accessible learning experience.

The problem

Several experiences in the Mind room asked visitors for uncomfortable postures: tilting the neck back, reaching for elements placed at awkward heights. Those conditions made the room hard to enjoy and, for people with reduced mobility, hard to use at all. Some modules also failed to translate complex ideas about how the brain works into something clear and playful, which limited their teaching value.

Who it is for

Visitors of Parque Explora, particularly children, older adults and people with reduced mobility. All of them move through the Mente room, a space that invites exploration of the brain across perception, emotion, thought and neural networks.

Final product
Context / Brief

The brief was to select a representative Ukelele product and develop packaging that reflected the brand’s warm, playful, and bohemian language while going beyond traditional packaging.

I chose one of the brand’s blankets and explored how its packaging could become part of the product experience, turning the act of opening it into a moment of discovery, warmth, and connection.

Tools
Illustrator · Canva · SolidWorks · Physical Prototyping
My role

I developed the packaging from concept to final prototype, exploring how the structure, graphics, and unboxing sequence could work together as one experience. My role included sketching, structural development, die-line design, graphic design, and physical prototyping.

Overview

Rather than treating the packaging as a container, I designed it as an extension of Ukelele’s storytelling. The concept transforms the packaging into a keepsake that gradually reveals the blanket and the memories associated with it.

The structure unfolds like an accordion, creating different layers of content as the user opens it. Each panel contributes to the experience through graphics, messages, and visual details inspired by the brand.

The result is a packaging experience that combines function, storytelling, and emotional design, turning a simple unboxing into a small family moment.

Process

The first sketches looked for a structure that could hold a storytelling: as you open the blanket with your family, lovely phrases appear one after another.

From there came the accordion pages with cuts for photographs, so that once the blanket is out, the packaging can stay in the home as a family album.

The unboxing experience

The packaging was designed to reveal itself gradually. Instead of opening as a conventional box, the accordion structure guides the user through a sequence of small discoveries.

As each layer unfolds, messages, graphics, and details appear alongside the blanket, transforming the packaging into a keepsake rather than something disposable.
